# OBGYN New-Patient Interview and Examination English Corpus

**Audience:** OBGYN physicians practicing clinic English as a second language.  
**Use:** Medical-English practice and interview design. These are patient-facing prompts, not a diagnostic or treatment protocol. Ask only questions relevant to the visit, then follow the patient’s answer. Use local consent, privacy, safeguarding, screening, and clinical protocols.

## 1. Opening and chief concern

- “What brings you in today?”
- “What is your main concern?”
- “When did this problem start?”
- “What were you doing when it started?”
- “Has it happened before?”
- “Is it getting better, worse, or staying the same?”
- “What worries you most about it?”
- “What would you like us to do today?”
- “Is there another concern you want to discuss?”
- “May I ask you some questions about your health and reproductive history?”
- “Would you like to speak with me alone?”
- “What name and pronouns would you like me to use?”
- “Would an interpreter or another communication support help?”

### Chief-complaint detail

Ask these one at a time, as relevant:

- “Where do you feel the problem?”
- “Does it move to another area?”
- “When did it begin?”
- “Did it begin suddenly or gradually?”
- “How long does it last?”
- “How often does it happen?”
- “How would you describe it?”
- “How severe is it, from zero to ten?”
- “What makes it worse?”
- “What makes it better?”
- “Is it related to your period, sex, urination, or bowel movements?”
- “What other symptoms happen at the same time?”
- “What have you tried so far?”
- “Did that help?”
- “How does this affect your daily life?”

### Urgent symptoms to clarify promptly

- “Could you be pregnant now?”
- “When did your last period start?”
- “Have you taken a pregnancy test?”
- “Are you bleeding now?”
- “How much blood are you losing?”
- “Are you feeling faint or short of breath?”
- “Do you have severe or sudden pain?”
- “Do you have a fever?”
- “Are you vomiting?”
- “Do you feel safe going home today?”

## 9. Condition-focused probes

These prompts are grouped by symptom pattern. They support history-taking across the conditions in the attached 138-entry reference. They do not establish a diagnosis. Use the patient’s symptoms and clinical judgment to select follow-up questions.

### A. Heavy, irregular, or unexpected bleeding

**Use for:** abnormal uterine bleeding; heavy menstrual bleeding; vaginal bleeding; postmenopausal bleeding; uterine or cervical polyps; cervical dysplasia or cancer; endometrial cancer; uterine sarcoma; bleeding related to pregnancy; and bleeding associated with fibroids or adenomyosis.

- “When did the bleeding start?”
- “Is it happening between periods, after sex, or after menopause?”
- “How many pads or tampons do you use in a day?”
- “Do you need to change them during the night?”
- “Do you pass clots?”
- “Have you felt weak, dizzy, or short of breath?”
- “Could you be pregnant?”
- “Do you take blood thinners or hormones?”
- “Have you had a similar problem before?”

### B. Period pain and chronic pelvic pain

**Use for:** menstrual cramps; adenomyosis; endometriosis, including extrapelvic or thoracic disease; chronic pelvic pain; ovarian remnant syndrome; pelvic floor pain; vulvodynia; and pain associated with intercourse.

- “Where is the pain?”
- “Does it happen before, during, or after your period?”
- “Does it happen every day or only at certain times?”
- “Does sex make the pain worse?”
- “Does passing urine or stool make the pain worse?”
- “Do you have pain in your chest or shoulder during your period?”
- “Have you had surgery for endometriosis or another pelvic condition?”
- “What treatments have you tried?”
- “Does the pain affect work, sleep, or sex?”

### C. Sudden pelvic pain, pregnancy possibility, or pregnancy loss

**Use for:** ectopic pregnancy; miscarriage; recurrent miscarriage; bleeding in pregnancy; pregnancy of unknown location; and acute pain with an adnexal mass.

- “When did the pain begin?”
- “Did it start suddenly?”
- “Is it stronger on one side?”
- “Do you have vaginal bleeding?”
- “When was your last period?”
- “Have you had a positive pregnancy test?”
- “Have you felt faint, weak, or had shoulder pain?”
- “Have you had an ectopic pregnancy or miscarriage before?”
- “Have you used fertility treatment?”

### D. Pelvic mass, cyst, or ovarian concern

**Use for:** adnexal tumors or masses; ovarian cysts; ovarian cancer; germ cell tumors; granulosa cell tumor; dermoid cyst; and other pelvic masses.

- “Have you noticed swelling or pressure in your abdomen?”
- “Do you feel full quickly when you eat?”
- “Have you had a change in appetite or weight?”
- “Do you need to urinate more often?”
- “Have you had new pelvic pain or bloating?”
- “Have you noticed abnormal bleeding?”
- “Has anyone in your family had ovarian or breast cancer?”
- “Have you had imaging that showed a mass or cyst?”
- “Has it changed on repeat imaging?”

### E. Vaginal discharge, itching, odor, or sores

**Use for:** bacterial vaginosis; yeast infection; trichomoniasis; vaginitis; cervicitis; sexually transmitted infections; lower genital tract dysplasia; and vulvar or vaginal skin conditions.

- “When did the discharge or irritation start?”
- “What does the discharge look or smell like?”
- “Do you have itching, burning, sores, or pain?”
- “Does it hurt when you urinate?”
- “Do you have pelvic pain or fever?”
- “Have you had a new sexual partner?”
- “Have you used antibiotics, vaginal medicines, or scented products?”
- “Have you had this problem before?”
- “Has a partner had symptoms or an STI?”

### F. Urinary symptoms

**Use for:** urinary tract infections; recurrent UTI; overactive bladder; urge, stress, or other urinary incontinence; urinary retention; bladder outlet obstruction; urethral diverticulum; and interstitial cystitis.

- “Does it burn when you urinate?”
- “Do you need to urinate more often or urgently?”
- “Do you leak urine when you cough, sneeze, lift, or exercise?”
- “Do you leak urine on the way to the bathroom?”
- “Do you have trouble starting or emptying your bladder?”
- “Do you feel that urine remains in your bladder?”
- “Have you seen blood in your urine?”
- “Do you have bladder pain when it fills?”
- “How many bladder infections have you had in the past year?”
- “Have you had fever, back pain, or vomiting with these symptoms?”

### G. Bulge, pressure, bowel, or pelvic floor symptoms

**Use for:** pelvic organ, uterine, vaginal, anterior, posterior, or post-hysterectomy prolapse; cystocele; rectocele; enterocele; pelvic floor disorders; fecal incontinence; and transvaginal mesh complications.

- “Do you feel or see a bulge in your vagina?”
- “Do you feel pressure or heaviness in your pelvis?”
- “Is it worse after standing or lifting?”
- “Do you need to press on the vagina or perineum to pass stool?”
- “Do you have trouble emptying your bladder or bowel?”
- “Do you leak stool or gas?”
- “Have you had pelvic surgery or mesh placement?”
- “Do you have pain, bleeding, or discharge from the vagina?”
- “Does the bulge affect walking, exercise, or sex?”

### H. Vulvar pain, skin change, or lesion

**Use for:** vulvodynia; lichen sclerosus; lichen planus; vulvar dysplasia or cancer; vaginal cancer; vaginal cysts; vaginal septum; vaginal agenesis; and imperforate hymen.

- “Where do you feel the pain or irritation?”
- “Does it hurt all the time or only when touched?”
- “Does sitting, urinating, or sex make it worse?”
- “Have you noticed a lump, sore, color change, or skin thickening?”
- “When did you first notice it?”
- “Has it grown, bled, or failed to heal?”
- “Have you had similar skin changes elsewhere?”
- “Have you used creams or other treatments?”
- “Have you had difficulty using a tampon or having a pelvic exam?”

### I. Fertility and pregnancy planning

**Use for:** infertility; female infertility; preconception care; PCOS; Müllerian or uterine anomalies; and fertility after cancer treatment or other medical care.

- “Are you trying to become pregnant now?”
- “How long have you been trying?”
- “How often do you have sex while trying to conceive?”
- “Have you been pregnant before?”
- “Have you had fertility testing or treatment?”
- “Are your periods regular?”
- “Have you had pelvic infections, endometriosis, or pelvic surgery?”
- “Has your partner had a fertility evaluation?”
- “Have you had chemotherapy, radiation, or a transplant?”
- “Do you have questions about pregnancy risks with your health conditions or medicines?”

### J. Pregnancy: antenatal symptoms and complications

**Use for:** routine prenatal care; preeclampsia or postpartum preeclampsia; gestational or pre-existing diabetes; hypertension; cholestasis; preterm labor; premature birth; rupture of membranes; placenta previa or accreta; Rh disease or atypical isoimmunization; thrombophilia; heart disease; and pregnancy after transplant.

- “How many weeks pregnant are you?”
- “When is your due date?”
- “Have you had any bleeding or fluid leaking?”
- “Are you having contractions or pelvic pressure?”
- “Is the baby moving as usual?”
- “Do you have a severe headache or changes in your vision?”
- “Do you have pain under your ribs, sudden swelling, or shortness of breath?”
- “Do you have itching, especially on your hands or feet?”
- “Have you been diagnosed with high blood pressure or diabetes?”
- “What medicines are you taking during pregnancy?”
- “Have you had a blood clot or heart problem?”
- “Have you had a transplant? Which organ, and when?”
- “Have you been told that your placenta is low or attached to a scar?”
- “Have you been told about your blood type or Rh status?”
- “Have you had an early birth or pregnancy complication before?”

### K. Pregnancy: fetal findings, twins, and fetal conditions

**Use for:** multiple gestation; fetal anemia or heart disease; macrosomia; conjoined twins; twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome (TTTS); twin anemia-polycythemia sequence (TAPS); twin reversed arterial perfusion (TRAP); amniotic band syndrome; congenital diaphragmatic hernia or CCAM; congenital high airway obstruction syndrome (CHAOS); pulmonary sequestration; spina bifida; sacrococcygeal teratoma; mediastinal teratoma; and other fetal findings.

- “What did the ultrasound or other test show?”
- “When was the finding first seen?”
- “Has it changed on later scans?”
- “Is this a single pregnancy or a multiple pregnancy?”
- “Have you been told whether the twins share a placenta?”
- “Have you met with maternal-fetal medicine or a genetic counselor?”
- “Have you had amniocentesis or another diagnostic test?”
- “What did the test results show?”
- “What questions or concerns do you have about the finding?”

### L. Pregnancy loss and cervical or uterine factors

**Use for:** miscarriage; recurrent miscarriage; incompetent cervix; septate or double uterus; Müllerian or other uterine anomalies; Asherman syndrome; and uterine isthmocele.

- “How many pregnancy losses have you had?”
- “At what week did each loss happen?”
- “Were any losses consecutive?”
- “Did you have pain, bleeding, fever, or a procedure?”
- “Have you had surgery or a procedure inside the uterus?”
- “Have you been told that your cervix opened early?”
- “Have you had a cerclage?”
- “Have you had imaging that showed a uterine shape or scar problem?”
- “Have you had difficulty becoming pregnant or carrying a pregnancy?”

### M. Breast symptom assessment

**Use for:** breast health assessment and breast-cancer screening, breast symptoms, and breast cancer risk.

- “Have you noticed a new breast lump?”
- “When did you first notice it?”
- “Has it changed in size?”
- “Do you have breast pain?”
- “Have you noticed a change in the skin or nipple?”
- “Is there discharge from the nipple?”
- “Have you had breast imaging or a biopsy?”
- “Has anyone in your family had breast or ovarian cancer?”
- “Have you had genetic testing for cancer risk?”

### N. Menopause and ovarian hormone symptoms

**Use for:** perimenopause; menopause; postmenopause; primary ovarian insufficiency; vaginal atrophy; and hormone therapy review.

- “When did your periods become irregular or stop?”
- “Have you had hot flashes or night sweats?”
- “Do you have vaginal dryness or pain with sex?”
- “Have you had urinary symptoms or repeated urinary infections?”
- “Have you had bleeding since your periods stopped?”
- “Did your periods stop before age 40?”
- “Have you had chemotherapy, ovarian surgery, or radiation?”
- “Do you use hormone therapy? Has it helped?”

### O. Cancer history or suspicious lesion

**Use for:** cervical, endometrial, ovarian, fallopian tube, peritoneal, vaginal, vulvar, and uterine cancers; dysplasia; germ cell or granulosa cell tumors; and cancer during pregnancy.

- “What symptoms led to the diagnosis or evaluation?”
- “When did the symptoms begin?”
- “What test or biopsy was done?”
- “What did the result show?”
- “Have you had surgery, chemotherapy, radiation, or other treatment?”
- “When was your last treatment?”
- “Are you having new bleeding, pain, bloating, or weight loss?”
- “Could you be pregnant now?”
- “Have you had genetic counseling or testing?”
- “Would you like to discuss how treatment may affect fertility or menopause?”

### P. Infection, pelvic infection, or upper abdominal pain

**Use for:** pelvic inflammatory disease; Fitz-Hugh–Curtis syndrome; cervicitis; sexually transmitted infections; and recurrent genital or urinary infections.

- “Do you have pelvic or lower abdominal pain?”
- “Do you have fever, chills, nausea, or vomiting?”
- “Do you have discharge or bleeding after sex?”
- “Do you have pain during sex?”
- “Do you have pain under your right ribs or in your shoulder?”
- “Have you had an STI or pelvic infection before?”
- “Were you and your partner treated?”
- “Could you be pregnant?”

### Q. Postpartum and postnatal concerns

**Use for:** postpartum or postnatal care; postpartum preeclampsia; peripartum cardiomyopathy; postpartum infection, bleeding, urinary or pelvic floor symptoms; and mental health concerns after birth.

- “When did you give birth?”
- “How was the birth?”
- “Are you having heavy bleeding, fever, or worsening pain?”
- “Do you have a severe headache, vision changes, chest pain, or shortness of breath?”
- “Do you have leg pain or swelling?”
- “Are you able to urinate and have bowel movements?”
- “Are you feeding the baby by breast, formula, or both?”
- “How is your mood?”
- “Do you feel able to care for yourself and the baby?”
- “Do you have support at home?”
- “Would you like to discuss contraception or future pregnancy?”

### R. Congenital, structural, or pediatric concerns

**Use for:** double uterus; Müllerian anomalies; septate uterus; uterine anomalies; vaginal agenesis or septum; imperforate hymen; pediatric neck masses; and congenital conditions listed in the fetal section.

- “When was the difference or problem first noticed?”
- “Have you had periods?”
- “Do you have monthly pain or pressure?”
- “Have you had trouble passing urine or stool?”
- “Have you had imaging or surgery?”
- “Have other family members had a similar condition?”
- “Has the condition affected puberty, periods, or fertility?”

### S. Prior procedures, fistula, mesh, or surgical complications

**Use for:** vesicovaginal or rectovaginal fistula; vaginal fistula; transvaginal mesh complications; post-hysterectomy prolapse; ovarian remnant syndrome; and uterine isthmocele.

- “What operation or procedure did you have?”
- “When was it done?”
- “What symptoms began after the procedure?”
- “Do you leak urine or stool through the vagina?”
- “Do you have pain, bleeding, discharge, or recurrent infections?”
- “Were you told that mesh was used?”
- “Have you had another procedure to treat this problem?”
- “Do you have the operative report or implant information?”

### T. Preventive and routine gynecology visit

**Use for:** well-woman or preventive visit; pelvic examination; cervical cancer screening; contraception counseling; and general reproductive health review.

- “Do you have a concern you want to discuss today?”
- “When was your last cervical cancer screening test?”
- “Were you told when you should have the next test?”
- “Do you have a history of abnormal results?”
- “Would you like to discuss contraception or pregnancy planning?”
- “Would you like STI testing?”
- “Do you have breast symptoms or questions about screening?”
- “Is there any preventive care you are due for?”

## 10. Examination language: consent and comfort

Use short instructions. Tell the patient what will happen before touching or moving them. Ask permission at each new step. Do not treat consent to one part of an exam as consent to every part.

- “I would like to examine your abdomen. Is that okay?”
- “I would like to examine your breasts. Is that okay?”
- “I would like to do a pelvic exam. Is that okay?”
- “I will explain each step before I begin.”
- “You can ask me to stop at any time.”
- “Please tell me if you feel pain.”
- “Would you like a chaperone in the room?”
- “Would you like a support person to stay with you?”
- “Do you have any questions before we begin?”
- “Would you like to pause?”
- “We can stop now.”
- “Are you ready for me to continue?”

## 13. Coverage note

Condition-focused probes above are organized by symptom pattern. Multiple conditions may share a probe set. The attached reference contains 138 condition and care records; these prompts are designed to elicit relevant history across that set. They do not replace examination, testing, referral, emergency assessment, or current local clinical guidance.

### U. Missed periods, irregular cycles, or androgen-related symptoms

**Use for:** amenorrhea; PCOS; hirsutism; primary ovarian insufficiency; and irregular bleeding related to absent ovulation.

- “When was your last period?”
- “Have your periods stopped or become irregular?”
- “Could you be pregnant?”
- “Have you had a major change in weight, exercise, stress, or eating?”
- “Have you noticed new hair growth on your face or body?”
- “Have you had new acne, scalp hair loss, or a deeper voice?”
- “Do you have milk-like fluid from your breasts when you are not breastfeeding?”
- “Do you have headaches or changes in your vision?”
- “Have you started or stopped any medicine or hormone?”

### V. Contraception and reproductive goals

**Use for:** contraception counseling and management; tubal ligation; pregnancy planning; and contraceptive concerns with medical conditions.

- “Do you want to avoid pregnancy now?”
- “Do you hope to become pregnant in the future?”
- “Which birth control methods have you used?”
- “How did each method work for you?”
- “Have you had side effects or trouble using a method?”
- “Do you prefer a daily, occasional, long-acting, or permanent method?”
- “Do you have high blood pressure, migraine with aura, blood clots, or smoke cigarettes?”
- “Would you like to discuss emergency contraception?”

### W. Cervical screening, HPV, or abnormal test results

**Use for:** HPV infection; abnormal Pap smear; cervical dysplasia; cervical polyps; cervical cancer; and cervical cancer screening.

- “When was your last Pap or HPV test?”
- “What did the result show?”
- “Have you had an abnormal result before?”
- “Did you have a colposcopy, biopsy, or treatment?”
- “Was follow-up testing recommended?”
- “Have you received the HPV vaccine?”
- “Do you have bleeding after sex or unusual discharge?”

### Y. Neck lump or mass

**Use for:** pediatric neck masses and other neck lumps included in the reference.

- “When did you first notice the lump?”
- “Has it changed in size?”
- “Is it painful?”
- “Have you had fever, sore throat, trouble swallowing, or trouble breathing?”
- “Have you had a recent infection?”
- “Has a clinician examined or imaged it before?”

### X. Metabolic, blood pressure, cardiac, or clotting concerns

**Use for:** gestational or pregestational diabetes; hypertension; preeclampsia; heart disease in pregnancy; peripartum cardiomyopathy; thrombophilia; fetal macrosomia; and pregnancy after transplant.

- “Do you have diabetes or high blood pressure?”
- “When were you diagnosed?”
- “What medicines do you take for it?”
- “Do you check your blood sugar or blood pressure at home?”
- “Have you had chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, or a racing heartbeat?”
- “Have you ever had a blood clot?”
- “Have you had a large baby or diabetes in a prior pregnancy?”
- “Do you have a heart, kidney, or transplant specialist?”
